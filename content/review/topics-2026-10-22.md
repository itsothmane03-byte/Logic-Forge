# Familiar topics and background knowledge review — 2026-10-22

This editorial pass read the passages and explanations in all 304 quiz items. It
checked whether the reasoning could be assessed without prior specialist
knowledge and simplified 40 passages. This is a readability and internal logic
review, not independent expert validation of every contextual factual claim.

## Metadata

Reasoning difficulty remains the existing `difficulty` value, 1–3. It is not a
measure of subject knowledge. Each item now separately declares
`backgroundKnowledge`:

- `general`: the reasoning uses ordinary concepts; no specialist prerequisite is
  needed to identify the relevant inference. Incidental topic details need not be
  understood in depth.
- `explained`: a domain concept or relevant rule is explained in the passage, so
  the reader can reason from the information supplied.
- `specialist`: outside subject knowledge is still necessary. These items should
  be excluded from ordinary practice until rewritten or offered in a clearly
  labelled advanced collection.

Current classification: **290 general, 14 explained, 0 specialist**. These are
editorial classifications, not a promise that every reader knows every word.
The unfamiliar-topic replacement remains useful in either of the first two
categories.

## Three broad interest groups

Groups overlap when an item has more than one tag. They express preferences,
not separate curricula or hard filters.

| Group | Existing tags | Items | Flawed / sound | Flawed skills / sound skills |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `everyday` | everyday, school, health, fitness, food, money, sports, parenting, local-news, shopping, travel, media, environment, housing | 257 | 193 / 64 | 38 / 37 |
| `work` | workplace | 67 | 47 / 20 | 34 / 20 |
| `tech` | tech, science | 74 | 59 / 15 | 34 / 13 |

Everyday examples cover every flawed skill, but no sound cherry-picking item
falls in that group. Work lacks flawed red herring, appeal to authority, appeal
to nature and cherry picking examples. Technology and science lacks flawed
appeal to emotion, appeal to tradition, anecdotal evidence and no true Scotsman
examples. There are larger gaps among sound counterparts.

Consequently, select the skill and reasoning difficulty first, avoid recently
used examples, then prefer a matching topic where a suitable example exists.
Topic preferences must not remove skills, change the sound/flawed balance, or
cause immediate repeats while fresh examples remain. A small fallback outside
the preferred interests is more honest than claiming complete topic coverage.

## Passage changes

The most important corrections supplied missing information inside the exercise:

- The server example now explicitly says any single failed part stops the whole
  server; readers need not know server architecture.
- The robot vacuum example explicitly offers camera mapping, so spotting its
  false two-way split does not depend on knowing vacuum models.
- Statistical significance is explained separately from effect size in both
  affected passages; the unexplained p-value is removed.
- The hundred-year-flood example supplies its per-year probability.
- Software examples replace unexplained PR, merge, deployment, staging,
  authentication-log, pipeline, hotfix and similar vocabulary with descriptions
  of the relevant actions. The caching example explains the term where it is
  used.
- Other changes clarify paid time off, tree specialists, foundation work,
  spreadsheet exports, high-intensity exercise, sunscreen protection and the
  relevant comparison groups in research.

- Every revised cue remains an exact substring of its passage.
- Content validation passes: 38 fallacies, 304 items, 228 flawed and 76 sound.
  Passages remain within the existing 140–420 character limit, with the current
  minimum 206 and maximum 408. Existing per-fallacy difficulty distributions hold.

For future content, require explicit metadata, put any indispensable subject
fact in the passage, and keep specialist items out of general practice. A new
interest should not be advertised as a complete track until its coverage is
measured across both flawed and sound examples.
