# Repair collection: AI content check, 2026-10-22

Claude authored the 15 starter exercises in `src/data/repairs.ts`. Codex reviewed the original arguments and three choices without the correctness labels, principles, or explanations. Its selected best choices matched the intended choices for all 15. This is a limited independent AI check, not human expert validation and not the earlier 304-question quiz review.

The follow-up review examined the explanations as well as the choices. Before publication, Codex revised:

- The statistical-significance example, removing the misleading descriptions of significance as a probability that the rise is real or not due to chance. The final repair asks about the size and practical value of the change.
- The study-app example, asking for comparative learning evidence and avoiding a blanket dismissal of popularity as having no informational value.
- The menu and lucky-socks examples, avoiding the implication that a simple comparison establishes causation.
- The late-homework and car explanations, clarifying uncertainty and the relevance of future costs and resale value.

The statistical correction follows the American Statistical Association's [statement on significance and p-values](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf), particularly principles 2 and 5. A significance label alone does not quantify the chance that a hypothesis is true, the size of an effect, or its practical importance.

Automated tests check library references, variety, unique IDs, exactly one best choice per exercise, distinct explanations, approximate option-length balance, shuffling, and selection without duplicates. Those checks cannot establish that every exercise is unambiguous. Further corrections are welcome through GitHub.
