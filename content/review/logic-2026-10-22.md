# Deduction collection review — 2026-10-22

Scope: the 18 fictional exercises in `src/data/logic.ts`, independently from the 304-question identification bank and 15 argument-repair exercises.

Each exercise states a few propositional facts and a claim. `src/engine/logic.ts` enumerates every assignment of its named statements, discards assignments that violate a fact, and classifies the claim across the remaining situations. Inconsistent facts or undefined statements are rejected. The same evaluator supplies optional examples; a single example illustrates possibility, not necessity.

Automated tests cover the six necessary truths, six necessary falsehoods, and six undecided claims, along with witness validity, consistent premises, unique IDs, and repeat-aware selection. A separate AI agent read every natural-language fact, claim, and explanation against its expression. It found no high-confidence mismatch. One explanation was clarified so “reserved or unreserved” describes separate possibilities, not a simultaneous contradiction.

This checks formal answers and internal consistency. It is not human expert validation, evidence of educational effectiveness, or a general claim about real-world certainty. The activity explicitly asks learners to treat its fictional facts as given and distinguishes possibility from probability.
